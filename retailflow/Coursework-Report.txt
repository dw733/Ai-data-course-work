The retailer's fulfillment manager should test one shared picking zone for five frequently
co-ordered jumbo-bag products, because the recorded orders identify a focused
opportunity to measure faster picking during busy periods.

Recommended action
After a current-data and space check, run a two-week picking-zone pilot for the red
retrospot jumbo bag and its four recurring companion products. Compare active picking
time and accuracy with the current arrangement.

Scope
This is a historical case study of positive purchase invoices from January-November 2011. The data identifies recurring
orders. It contains no warehouse locations, walking distances or measured picking times. The proposed benefit must be
tested.

PHASE 2 - SELECTION METHOD

Choose the five products before
checking later orders
A basket is one purchase invoice. A product is present once in that basket even if the
quantity is large. This prevents one bulk order from counting as many separate
co-purchases.
Step

Selection rule using January-August only

1. Screen common
products

Start with the thirty products appearing in the most UK baskets.

2. Screen recurring
pairs

Require at least 100 shared baskets and lift of at least 2. Rank by shared-basket
count, then lift, then stock codes.

3. Find an anchor

Take the highest-ranked pair. Use its more frequent product as the anchor: the red
retrospot jumbo bag.

4. Form a small pilot

Add the anchor's four strongest eligible companion products. This defines five
products.

5. Check the fixed
group

Recount the same products in September-November. Do not use those later counts to
change the selected list.

Why use lift in the screen?
Two popular items may overlap simply because both are common. Lift divides observed
shared baskets by an independence benchmark. A value of 2 means twice that benchmark,
not twice the sales effect of a promotion. The formula and exact pair values appear in
Appendix D.
These are practical screening rules. The five-product limit keeps the proposed pilot small;
it is not an optimized warehouse solution. The top-thirty screen can miss useful niche
products. The later period checks persistence, while repeat customers and seasonality can
still link the two periods.

PHASE 2 - WORKLOAD OVER TIME

November handled about twice the
earlier daily volume

Chart description: Line chart of UK purchase invoices per calendar day in January-November 2011. Earlier months are muted,
September-November is lightly shaded, the dashed January-August reference is directly labelled 40.4 per day, and November
is directly labelled 82.7.

The UK subset contains 9,815 invoices across the 243 calendar days in January-August.
That is 40.4 invoices per calendar day. November contains 2,481 invoices across 30 days,
or 82.7 per calendar day. The ratio is 2.05.
Each month uses all its calendar days, including days with no retained invoices.
Normalization avoids treating a longer month as automatically busier. Calendar days are
not warehouse working days, and invoice counts do not measure staff utilization.

Operating implication
A busier order-entry period makes a focused efficiency test relevant. The graph does not
establish that the warehouse was slow. A pilot must record actual pick times, staffing and
replenishment conditions before any claim of improvement.

PHASE 2 - NETWORK

The network shows purchases together,
not warehouse routes

Chart description: NetworkX graph uses readable product names inside five nodes. Teal anchor links and a thicker teal
outline focus the red jumbo bag. Other relationships are light grey. Edge width encodes shared discovery invoices, and node
area encodes displayed weighted degree plus a base.

Each node represents one selected product. An undirected edge joins two products
appearing on the same discovery-period invoice. Edge width represents shared invoices.
Node area uses a small base plus displayed weighted degree, which sums the incident
edge weights (NetworkX Developers, n.d.).
The red bag uses teal and a thicker outline. Product names sit inside their nodes, while teal
links connect to the anchor. The five-product display keeps the group easy to identify and
avoids a dense catalogue-wide diagram.
The layout is schematic. It supplies no evidence about current shelf locations, physical
distance, walking paths or the order in which a worker collects products. Multiple edges
may refer to the same invoice. The edge list and product key accompany the data.

PHASE 2 - HIERARCHY AND SCOPE

A small pilot covers about one in ten UK
invoices

Chart description: An editable-data tree partitions 17,406 invoices into 15,716 UK and 1,690 international. UK leaves are
12,918 with none of the selected products, 1,234 with one, and 1,564 with at least two. The pilot leaf is teal and labelled 10.0
percent.

Across the complete retained period, 1,564 distinct UK invoices contain at least two
selected products. That is 10.0% of the 15,716 UK invoices. It is a historical count of
potentially relevant purchase tasks, not a count of minutes saved.
The hierarchy first separates UK and international invoices. It then partitions UK invoices
into no selected products, exactly one selected product, and two or more. Each invoice
belongs to exactly one leaf and all leaves reconcile to their parent.
Orders with only one selected product can still be affected by moving that product, while
replenishment staff may face extra work. The trial should therefore track wider handling
effects, not only the multi-product orders used to justify the pilot.

PHASE 2 - MULTIVARIATE VIEW

Product value and price describe a
different operating question

Chart description: Two scatterplot facets share both scales and divide products by median unit price. Context points are
muted. An outlined point and direct red-bag label identify the anchor without colour alone.

Each point represents a UK product. The horizontal axis gives invoice frequency and the
vertical axis gives retained gross purchase value. Two panels separate median recorded
unit prices below GBP 3 from GBP 3 or above, using identical scales. An outline identifies
the red jumbo bag.
Frequency, purchase value and unit price provide three variables in one faceted view.
Products with fewer invoices can have substantial gross value because quantities and
prices differ. A high-value product is not automatically the best product to move into a
shared picking zone.
Gross purchase value is quantity multiplied by price for retained positive lines. It excludes
cancellation deductions and product costs. The analysis uses co-purchase relationships to
choose a trial group, then requires observed warehouse time and accuracy to judge the
trial.

PHASE 5 - OPERATING PILOT

A two-week trial should measure time
and accuracy
Stage

Proposed operating step

Before the trial

Refresh the product list using current orders. Confirm current picking locations, zone
capacity and replenishment needs. Agree a minimum useful improvement and the
measurement rules.

Ten working days

If the layout permits, retain normal locations and put limited pilot stock in the shared
zone. Randomly assign eligible tasks to current or zone picking, balanced across pickers
and shifts. Keep task contents comparable.

Primary outcome

Active pick seconds per eligible invoice, measured from a consistent start and finish.
Compare task distributions and report uncertainty, rather than only one average.

Guardrails

Wrong-item rate, missing-item/stockout events and replenishment labour. Check
handling effects for single-product orders as well.

Decision

Keep the change only if the measured time benefit meets the agreed operating target
without unacceptable error or replenishment harm. Otherwise revise or stop the trial.

Winning acceptance
Start with the familiar product ranking, then show the companion counts. Let the manager
inspect actual current locations before asking for a change. Demonstrate the limited
product list and a reversible trial rather than a warehouse-wide redesign.

Accessibility
Slides use at least 18 pt text, one font and editable charts. The palette was checked with
colour-vision simulations. Labels, position and outlines preserve meaning without colour.
Each report chart has a written description.

RECOMMENDATION AND REFERENCES

Approve one measured picking-zone
pilot
The records show a busier historical order-entry period and a small set of products
repeatedly purchased together. The later-period check supports keeping the same group
under consideration, while its lower share cautions against assuming constant demand.
The requested decision is one two-week trial for the five-product group after a current-data
and physical-layout check. No warehouse change or time saving has been observed in this
project. The trial turns the descriptive pattern into a question the fulfillment manager can
answer.
